Acceptable use policy

Version 1 · 25 November 2026.

Pre-release draft, not legal advice. This policy is part of the InFlight terms. It applies to everything you put into InFlight's online features: shared projects, cloud storage, client galleries, comments and messages. Photos that stay on your own device are never scanned or seen by us.

You must not use InFlight to

  • store, share or request any image that sexually exploits or abuses a child, or that sexualises a child in any way;
  • share intimate images of anyone without their consent, or threaten to;
  • share content that is illegal where you or the viewer are, including terrorist content, or content that encourages serious violence or self-harm;
  • harass, threaten, stalk or bully anyone, or share someone's private information to harm them;
  • infringe copyright, trade marks or other rights, for example by sharing other people's photographs as your own;
  • photograph or share people in ways that break privacy law where you are;
  • impersonate someone, or mislead people about who you are;
  • send spam, malware or phishing through invitations, galleries or comments;
  • attack, overload, probe or get round the security, limits or licence checks of InFlight, or access accounts or data that are not yours;
  • resell or run InFlight's online services for others without our written agreement.

How we keep shared and cloud photos safe

  • Photos uploaded to InFlight's cloud or published in a shared or client gallery may be checked with automated tools: matching against lists of known child sexual abuse images, and automated detection of other clearly unsafe content.
  • A match with known child sexual abuse material blocks the upload, keeps the evidence securely and is reported, as the law requires, to the National Crime Agency in the UK and to the US National Center for Missing & Exploited Children, which passes reports to police worldwide. The account is closed.
  • An automated flag of other content blocks sharing until a person reviews it. It is never reported automatically.
  • Photos on your own device, and photos sent to your own storage or your own AI key, are never scanned by us.

Not live yet. The automated checks above are being built and will be switched on before shared projects, client galleries and cloud storage open to the public.

Report content or an account

Anyone can report content they think is illegal or breaks this policy, including people without an account. Use the contact form or email david@djleitch.co.uk, and include:

  • the gallery or project link, or what you saw and where;
  • why you think it is illegal or breaks this policy;
  • your email, so we can tell you what we did (you can stay anonymous, but then we cannot reply).

If a child is in immediate danger, contact the police first. In the UK you can also report child sexual abuse images to the Internet Watch Foundation.

We look at reports promptly, and quickly for anything involving children or a risk of harm. We may remove content, revoke a gallery or suspend an account. We tell the person who reported it, and the person whose content we acted on, what we decided and why, unless the law or someone's safety prevents it. Either of you can ask us to look again by replying to that message.

Point of contact

For users and for authorities, including under the UK Online Safety Act and the EU Digital Services Act: david@djleitch.co.uk. We can deal with requests in English.

Breaking this policy

Depending on how serious it is, we may warn you, remove content, revoke galleries or shared access, suspend or close your account, and report to the police where the law requires or someone is at risk. See section 11 of the terms for how suspension works and what it never touches.
