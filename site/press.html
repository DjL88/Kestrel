About InFlight

Background, facts and assets for anyone writing about InFlight — a review, a roundup, a launch note or a mention in passing. Use anything on this page freely.

Boilerplate

InFlight is a calm, privacy-first photo workspace for importing, culling, editing and exporting — built so photographers spend more time behind the camera and less time managing files. It works fully offline, never changes or uploads an original photograph, and keeps every edit as a reversible step. Basic is free with no account; InFlight Pro is a one-off licence, not a subscription. InFlight runs on Windows, macOS and the web today, with iOS and Android to follow.

The story

InFlight started from an ordinary complaint: importing, culling and editing a day's shoot takes longer than the shoot itself, and most of that time is spent fighting the tool rather than the photographs. InFlight is built around a single rule — an original file is never changed, moved or replaced. Everything InFlight does to a photo is stored as a small, reversible recipe next to it, so a photographer can always get back to exactly what the camera captured.

That carefulness extends to what InFlight tells people. It says plainly when it is showing a RAW file's embedded preview rather than a full sensor decode, never invents detail in a photograph, and keeps an uncertain photo for review rather than silently rejecting it. On-device machine learning can suggest a species, a keeper or a mask, and a verified Pro account can ask for a second opinion from a cloud model — but every suggestion is the photographer's to check, change or ignore, and nothing runs without the person's say.

InFlight is also designed to be calm: a quiet screen, plain language, full keyboard access, reduced motion, and nothing that relies on colour alone — built with colour-blind, ADHD and autistic photographers specifically in mind. It works the same way on every platform it supports, so a workflow learned on a laptop carries straight over to a phone.
